BEFORE YOU SCROLL

This case study isn't just about designing the ideal flow.

It's also about designing the best possible experience inside real business decisions, real technical limits, and knowing when to challenge a request versus when to protect it inside its constraints.

So this isn't a walkthrough of every screen. The flows a cinema site needs are easy enough to picture, and I had about three weeks to design them all. What's worth showing is what happened while designing them. These are some moments.

My role

I was responsible for UX, and for holding the project together on both sides of it.

Externally I led the design team: gathering requirements, hearing out the backend team on what the system could support, and presenting the outcomes to the client directly.

Internally I managed the schedule for myself and the visual designers, and followed up through each stage so the work stayed on track against the opening date.

The Process

Looking back: stages 1–2 went well — requirements and constraints were mapped early, so nothing structural surfaced late. Stages 3–4 had room to improve; designing and aligning in parallel left less time to pressure-test flows before handoff.

01

Cross-Cultural UX

When a brand brings a service into a new market, it brings its own concept of the business with it. That concept was built on how customers behave somewhere else.

Designing across cultures means finding where the model and the local user don't meet, saying so even when the model is already decided, and then compromising well — protecting what the brand came in with while closing the gap it leaves open.

INITIAL DESIGN

Proposed removing self-declared discounts, standard rate + in-person ID check, to reduce fraud risk in the VN market.

THE CHALLENGE

The Client kept the honour-system self-selection to preserve booking speed and chain-wide consistency, accepting the risk.

SOLUTION — WORKING INSIDE IT

I added clear category guidelines + explicit policy disclaimer to reduce misuse within the model, rather than changing it

Balancing UX with Technical Constraints

A flow that reads well on paper still has to fit the system that will run it. When the data can't take the shape the design assumes, the design is what moves.

The work is knowing which part of the experience the constraint actually costs, then paying that cost back somewhere the system doesn't control.

INITIAL DESIGN

Size and Flavor as two separate grid steps, for lower cognitive load

STEP 1 — SIZE

STEP 2 — FLAVOR + PRICES
THE CHALLENGE

The legacy POS/inventory system only supports a flat data structure, not nested modifiers.

Two dependent steps were not something the data behind the menu could express. And modifying the existing system wasn't an option due to the time constraint.

SOLUTION

I collapsed the two steps into one: flattened radio-button options representing pre-combined [Size + Flavor + Price] strings.

Compensated on the UX layer instead of the data layer:

1. Grouped options so the flat list still read as size-then-flavor logic visually

2. Showed price deltas transparently next to each option (e.g. +18,000đ)

3. Set the base combination as the default, so most users finish in one tap

03

Transforming a Risky Client Request into a Seamless Operation

A client asks for a feature, but what they're describing is an operational problem. Taking the request literally can ship the risk along with it.

So the question isn't whether to build it — it's what the request is trying to solve, and which interaction solves that without putting the outcome in the wrong hands.

CLIENT REQUEST

Add an "I'm here" button directly on the e-ticket, letting customers self-trigger the kitchen to start preparing their F&B order on arrival.

THE CHALLENGE

Users could easily tap the button too early or by accident.

And there was no reliable way to confirm the customer's actual location without GPS, which introduced cost concern and time constrain.

SOLUTION — WORKING INSIDE IT

I proposed a different interaction model that solved the operational problem the client actually had and kitchen timing
