02 — APP UX IMPROVEMENT HONDA VIETNAM

Improving the everyday UX of My Honda+ through research-driven redesign

Mobile app UX design Heuristic analysis Usability testing
OVERVIEW

My Honda+ had a broad set of features for vehicle owners, but usability issues across key flows made some features difficult to understand and use. I led UX research and redesign across vehicle registration, the My Vehicle page, and engagement features, working with Honda Vietnam to validate the direction and feasibility.

The Problems

The app had useful features, but users couldn't always understand how they worked, what would happen after an interaction, or why they should return to them.

  1. 01

    Users struggled to add their vehicle

  2. 02

    The My Vehicle page didn't surface information in a way users expected

  3. 03

    Favorite / Follow / Like didn't communicate their purpose or outcome.

01
At the crucial entrance feature, which is the Add a Vehicle page

Users couldn't find their likely most used method and bypassed the app's guidance entirely

Out of 8 users, 4 were unable to add vehicles successfully due to the number digit of VIN. Users experienced difficulty identifying the frame number, and some completed the task without referring to the provided instructions. The current instructions may not effectively help users locate or identify the required vehicle information.

WE BELIEVE THAT WE SHOULD

Prioritize the method of adding a vehicle that users find most convenient in practice.

Research showed that users preferred using information from their registration paper, so I added "By Registration Paper" as an alternative way to find the VIN.

And users loved it.

WE ALSO IMPROVED UX BY

Adding a progress bar, progressive info revealing guide users step by step without overwhelming them, providing a jargon explainer make it easier for even new bike holders.

1
Progress bar

Splitting the flow into distinct steps makes the process easier to follow and reduces cognitive load.

2
Progressive disclosure

Guidance for the selected method is revealed step-by-step, so users are guided without being overwhelmed upfront.

3
Jargon explainer

A tappable prompt clarifies technical terms, making the flow more approachable for first-time bike owners.

4
Built-in support

A "Contact nearest Honda dealer for support" option gives users a way forward if they get stuck, instead of leaving them to fail silently.

VEHICLE PRICE & COLOR

During usability testing, only 1 of 8 users completed these tasks smoothly. Most users either couldn't find the interaction or misunderstood what would happen after tapping it.

The issue was not simply that users couldn't find the buttons. The interactions didn't match users' existing mental models.

Favorite
Users didn't understand why they would save a dealer or where to find saved dealers later.
Follow
The interaction didn't clearly communicate that it was tappable or what would happen after tapping.
Like
Users interpreted the feature as a bookmark and expected to receive information about the selected vehicle color later.
OUR SOLUTIONS WERE

Use familiar interaction patterns and make the outcome of every action clear.

WHAT I CHANGED
1
Clear feedback

Added confirmation after each action so users could understand what happened and where the result was saved.

2
Explain the value

Added a short first-use explanation to clarify what saving a dealer does and surfaced the saved dealers where users would expect to find them.

3
Use familiar conventions

Replaced ambiguous icons with more recognizable patterns, such as a bookmark for saving and a heart for liking.

My reflection

This is the first project where I have worked with many stakeholders, especially the backend team.

What went well. Testing every direction with real owners kept the team honest. When the research showed that riders reach for their registration licence rather than a frame number, we changed the default instead of defending the original flow, and the client agreed because the evidence was in front of them.

What I would do differently. I would have brought the frontend developer into the research sessions earlier. We confirmed technical feasibility after the improvement directions were drawn, which meant reworking details we could have settled in the first week.
